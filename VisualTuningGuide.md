## What Increasing & Decreasing Gains Does to the Graph

### Proportional Gain (*kP*)

*kP* multiplies raw positional error (`P_out = kP * error`). It acts as the primary accelerator.

- **Increasing *kP*:**
  - **Initial Peak:** Drives the starting output spike higher, demanding more initial launch power.
  - **Slope:** Steepens the line dropping toward zero.
  - **If set too high:** Generates too much kinetic energy, causing the output curve to cross zero and dip deep into negative territory (reverse braking) or oscillate.

- **Decreasing *kP*:**
  - **Initial Peak:** Drops the initial launch voltage spike.
  - **Slope:** Creates a gentler, more gradual descent toward zero.
  - **If set too low:** The output line flattens out above zero before reaching setpoint, leaving the robot stranded due to static friction (stiction).



### Derivative Gain (*kD*)

*kD* calculates the rate of change of error (`D_out = kD * (Δ error) / Δ t`). It acts as the dynamic brake.

- **Increasing *kD*:**
  - **Step Zero Kick:** Produces a sharp initial spike at frame 1 when error changes instantaneously.
  - **Braking Curve:** Pulls the descending curve flat as it nears zero, preventing the output from crossing into negative numbers.
  - **If set too high:** Amplifies discrete encoder tick quantization noise, creating jagged "sawtooth" teeth along the slope and forcing motors to chatter through gear backlash.


- **Decreasing *kD*:**
  - **Noise Reduction:** Smooths out jagged, staircased output signals into a continuous line.
  - **Step Zero Kick:** Reduces or eliminates initial voltage spikes.
  - **If set too low:** Fails to counteract chassis momentum, letting the robot blow past setpoint and forcing *kP* to fire reverse power.

## Diagnostic Guide by Graph Profile

### 1. Overshoot (Negative Output Dip)

- **Visual Profile:** The line descends toward zero, crosses below the *0* axis into negative output values (e.g., *-40* to *-200*), and then slowly climbs back up to settle on zero.
- **Physical Cause:** The chassis carried too much linear/angular momentum past the target position. The negative output indicates the PID controller is actively firing the motors in reverse to pull the robot back.
- **How to Fix:**
  - **Increase *kD*:** Adds derivative braking to flatten the curve right onto zero before it crosses under.
  - **Lower *kP*:** If raising *kD* introduces high-frequency chatter teeth, reduce *kP* instead to lower the approach velocity entering the braking zone.


### 2. Undershoot / Stiction Stall (Positive Flatline)

- **Visual Profile:** Output slopes downward normally, but then levels out and forms a horizontal flatline at a positive value (e.g., *+20* to *+50*) above zero, staying there indefinitely.
- **Physical Cause:** The robot stopped short of its setpoint. Because the remaining positional error is small, `kP * error` calculates a low motor output (e.g., *+30*) that is insufficient to overcome static tire friction (stiction).

- **How to Fix:**
  - **Increase *kP*:** Raises the baseline power generated by micro-errors so the motors overcome static friction.
  - **Add Anti-Windup Integral (*kI*):** If *kP* is already well-tuned for high speeds, set a tiny *kI* with an anti-windup range equal to the stall zone (e.g., *2.0"*) to accumulate residual error and nudge the chassis onto setpoint.


### 3. Oscillation (Low-Frequency Waves vs. High-Frequency Chatter)

- **Low-Frequency Waves (Slow Overshoot Cycles):**
  - **Visual Profile:** Large, smooth sine waves swinging rhythmically above and below zero across multiple tenths of a second.
  - **Physical Cause:** *kP* is too strong relative to *kD*. The robot continuously over-corrects past the setpoint in both directions.
  - **How to Fix:** Reduce *kP* significantly and re-establish a stable curve before adjusting *kD*.

- **High-Frequency Chatter (Sawtooth / Staircasing):**
  - **Visual Profile:** Rapid, jagged "teeth" or discrete staircase steps interrupting the slope during acceleration or deceleration.
  - **Physical Cause:** *kD* is reacting to discrete sensor quantization ticks (*\Delta e*) or gear backlash slop.
  - **How to Fix:**
    1. Reduce *kD* until the jagged teeth disappear.
    2. Add a mild slew rate (e.g., `10`–`15`) to limit maximum voltage change per millisecond.
